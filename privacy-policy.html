Who this policy covers

This policy applies to Feldman Clock (com.feldman.clock), published by Feldman. It explains what the app stores, the two optional network requests it can make, and the choices available to you. Effective September 1, 2026.

Data collection and sharing

Feldman Clock does not collect, transmit, sell, or share personal or sensitive user data. The app contains no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, no user accounts, and no behavioral tracking.

Because the app does not collect personal data, Feldman does not retain personal data on a server and has no personal server-side profile to delete.

What stays on your device

All of the following is stored only in the app's private storage and is never transmitted to Feldman:

  • Alarms, including labels, times, repeat schedules and chosen ringtones
  • Timers and stopwatch state, including lap times
  • Your saved world-clock cities
  • All settings, themes, standby configuration and widget configuration

This data is included in Android's system backup if you have backup enabled in your device settings. That backup goes to your own Google account under Google's terms — Feldman has no access to it. You can disable it in Settings → Google → Backup.

What leaves your device

Feldman Clock makes network requests for exactly two optional features. Both work only if you choose to use them, and the app is fully functional without either.

1. Weather (optional)

If you grant the location permission, the clock screen shows current conditions. An approximate latitude and longitude is sent to Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com). The app requests coarse location only — roughly city-level — and never requests fine or GPS location. For cities you add yourself, the city name is sent to geocoding-api.open-meteo.com to resolve its coordinates. Open-Meteo states that it requires no API key and does not track users; see their terms.

If you deny the location permission, the app works normally and simply does not show local weather. You can revoke it at any time in Android Settings → Apps → Clock → Permissions.

2. World-clock city list (optional)

When you search for a city to add, the app downloads a public, static city and timezone list from raw.githubusercontent.com. This is an ordinary HTTPS request for a public file. Your search text is never sent — filtering happens on your device after the list is downloaded. GitHub will see your IP address, as it would for any web request.

No other network requests are made. All traffic uses HTTPS, and the app's network security configuration rejects plain-text connections outright.

Sharing with other Feldman apps

Feldman Clock can expose some of its data to other Feldman apps installed on the same device, so that Feldman Launcher can show running timers and Feldman Home can manage your alarms.

  • Running and paused timers — label, state and remaining time — can be read by Feldman Launcher.
  • Alarms — time, label, repeat schedule and enabled state — can be read and changed by Feldman Home.

These interfaces are protected at the signature level, meaning only apps signed with the same key as Feldman Clock can use them. No other app on your device can read this data, and none of it travels over a network or reaches Feldman.

Retention and deletion

Everything the app stores lives in Android app storage on your device. It remains until you delete it in the app, clear Feldman Clock's storage, or uninstall the app. You can also export your alarms and settings to a file of your own from Settings → Backup & Restore; that file goes wherever you choose to save it and is never uploaded.

Security and third-party code

The app relies on Android platform services and open-source libraries, and uses no third-party advertising or analytics SDKs. Local data is protected by Android's app sandbox. Network traffic is HTTPS-only, enforced by the app's network security configuration.

Children's privacy

Feldman Clock is a general-audience utility. It is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect personal information from children or anyone else.

Changes and contact

If the app's data practices change, this policy will be updated before those practices take effect, and the effective date will be revised.

Questions or privacy requests can be submitted through the project's GitHub issue tracker. The app source is available in the Feldman Clock repository.
